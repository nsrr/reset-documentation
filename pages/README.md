## About

The Regulation of Emotions, Sleep Extension, and mTBI (RESET) study is a within-subjects counterbalanced clinical trial, funded by the National Institutes of Health (NIH) and National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) grant R15MH136612, conducted from 2024 to present. The study targets individuals aged 18-35 years of all sexes who have recently sustained a mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI), with a targeted follow-up duration of 10 days. Its main aim is to examine the preliminary efficacy of a mid-day nap to improve emotion regulation in this population and to determine whether a midday nap alters top-down neural control of emotion regulation.

The RESET dataset offers a comprehensive multimodal collection of physiological, behavioral, and self-report data investigating the effect of naps on emotion regulation following recent mTBI in young adults. Data includes polysomnography from nap and nocturnal sleep, wrist actigraphy, eye tracking, pupillometry, ERP, and a suite of validated questionnaires assessing sleep quality, concussion symptoms, and emotional control. This dataset supports studies on sleep’s role in emotional and cognitive recovery post brain injury, with high-quality phenotyping and sleep monitoring across multiple modalities.

The primary outcomes investigated include self-reported emotion regulation, behavioral measures of emotion regulation, executive control over emotions via pupillometry, and executive control over emotions via neurophysiology (ERP). Secondary outcomes involve sleep duration and sleep patterns assessed by actigraphy, as well as self-report control measures. The primary exposure of interest is mTBI.

Participants for the RESET study were recruited based on the following criteria: Between the ages of 18 and 35 years old; have normal or corrected-to-normal vision; and sustained a first or second concussion within the last 2-3 weeks. Exclusion criteria: Under 18 or over 35 years old; not having corrected normal vision with glasses or contacts; having an astigmatism in both eyes; had a concussion over 3 weeks ago; has a sleep disorder; is a shift worker.

## Methods

### Data Collection

The study involved two data collection visits and one phone contact. Participants followed a within-subjects counterbalanced design, completing an emotion regulation task after both a nap and a no-nap (wake) condition, with the two conditions completed one week apart.

At the initial lab session, participants completed demographic information and several questionnaires. Additional questionnaires, including concussion symptom questionnaires, emotional and cognitive questionnaires, and sleep quality assessments, were administered at different timepoints throughout the RESET study. Behavioral and physiological tasks were conducted during lab sessions. Nocturnal PSG was collected on the night prior to and the night after each laboratory session, totaling 4 nights. Nap PSG was conducted during nap opportunities in the lab.

#### Questionnaires

Questionnaires were administered at different timepoints throughout the study:

- **Beginning of Visit 1:** Demographic information, Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), Rivermead Post-Concussion Symptoms Questionnaire (RPQ), Cognitive Emotion Regulation Questionnaire (CERQ), and Behavioral Activation/Inhibition Scales (BAS/BIS).

- **Before and after each nap/restful wake bout (Visits 1 and 2):** Stanford Sleepiness Scale (SSS) and Positive and Negative Affect Schedule (PANAS).

- **After eye tracking (Visits 1 and 2):** PANAS and Post-Concussion Symptom Scale (PCSS).

- **After eye tracking (Visit 1 only):** UPPS-P Impulsive Behavior Scale

- **Daily for 10 days:** PCSS and Consensus Sleep Diary (CSD).

#### Physiological and Behavioral data

**Eye Tracking**

Measurements taken during lab sessions as part of emotion regulation task Measurements of total fixation count, average total dwell time, proportion of interest area fixations, proportion of interest area dwell time, fixation count outside the area of interest, and average dwell time outside the area of interest, using SR EyeLink 1000 Plus.

**Pupillometry**

Measurements taken during lab sessions Measurements of pupil size (area in mm), with proportional change calculated across early (first 2.5 seconds) and late (last 2.5 seconds) aggregated time points, using SR EyeLink 1000 Plus.

**ERP (event-related potentials)**

ERP via EEG taken during lab sessions. EEG data from Pz, measuring average amplitude of positive deflection, processed using BrainVision Analyzer 2 software.

### Nap and Nocturnal Polysomnography (PSG) Collection

#### Nap PSG

**Devices Used for Naps:** BrainVision EasyCaps (Brain Products, GmbH, Germany). These were used for attended PSG recordings during nap sessions. Study Type: Type I (attended studies with full sleep staging).

**Signals Captured (Channels):** A 32-electrode cap montage was applied, consisting of 25 cortical EEG electrodes (F3, F4, Fz, F7, F8, FCz, FC1, FC2, FC5, FC6, Cz, C3, C4, CP1, CP2, CP5, CP6, Pz, P3, P4, P7, P8, POz, O1, O2), 2 EOG (eye movements), and 2 EMG (muscle activity) electrodes. Additionally, 2 reference electrodes were placed on the mastoid bones, and a ground electrode was used. Sampling rate recorded at 500 Hz, scored at 256 Hz.

**Scoring System and Software:** All PSG recordings of naps were analyzed using BrainVision Analyzer (Brain Products, GmbH, Germany). Scoring was performed by hand according to the American Academy of Sleep Science guidelines.

**Scoring Rules and Definitions:**

- Sleep stages (naps): PSG recordings were coded for sleep stages using BrainVision Analyzer 2 software according to the American Academy of Sleep Science. Time in sleep stages (SWS and REM) was reported.

- Delta power (ΔSWS): Defined with a range of 0.5–4Hz. This was extracted from SWS staged segments using the periodogram method for spectral analysis, with data divided into 4-second epochs for artifact rejection and a fast-Fourier transform (FFT) applied using a Hanning window with 10% overlap and utilizing covariance.

- Theta power (ΘREM): Defined with a range of 4–8Hz. This was extracted from REM sleep staged segments using the periodogram method for spectral analysis, with data divided into 4-second epochs for artifact rejection and a fast-Fourier transform (FFT) applied using a Hanning window with 10% overlap and utilizing covariance.

#### Nocturnal PSG (Home sleep)

Home based data collection included four night of sleep physiology recordings’ using Advanced Brain Monitoring Sleep Profiler™ EEG Sleep Monitor headbands.

**Study Type:** Type IV (unattended home sleep test with a minimum of 3 channels, allowing direct calculation of AHI or RDI).

**Signals Captured (Channels):** Three frontopolar channels (ground, AF7, and AF8 according to the international 10–20 system) to record EEG, in addition to EOG and EMG signals for sleep staging. Sampling Rate: 256 Hz.

**Scoring System and Software:** Sleep Profiler data was coded for sleep stages using the validated Sleep Profiler auto-staging software.

- Sleep stages (night): Sleep Profiler data was coded for sleep stages using the validated Sleep Profiler auto-staging. Sleep staging done via validated auto-staging algorithm and reviewed by PIs for artifacts/errors. Sleep stages identified per AASM criteria. Sleep stages and spectral analyses performed for delta power (0.5–4 Hz) and theta power (4–8 Hz). Measures of interest included TST and the percentage of the night spent in SWS and REM sleep specifically.

- Sleep period determination (lights on/off): For both nap and nocturnal PSG, lights-out time was determined based on “Paperwork”.

### Actigraphy Collection

Wrist-worn Philips Respironics Spectrum Plus Actiwatch worn on the non-dominant wrist continuously for 10 days. Sampling frequency at 32 Hz; data collected in 15-second epochs. Data downloaded and exported via Philips Respironics Actiware software.

**Sleep Diaries:** Participants completed the Consensus Sleep Diary ([Carney et al., 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3250369/)) daily, in conjunction with wearing the Actigraph. This allowed for more accurate scoring of actigraph data as per standard recommendations.

**Actigraphy Scoring:** Sleep intervals scored using a combination of consensus sleep diary, participant button presses, and Actiware’s validated algorithm detecting nocturnal and daytime sleep. Research assistants trained and supervised by PIs for scoring consensus.

Primary actigraphy outcomes include: Average total sleep time (TST), Sleep Efficiency (SE), Sleep onset (SOn) and offset (SOff) variability (standard deviation), Nap habituality (average number of naps), changes in nocturnal sleep following nap days vs no-nap days.

## Data de-identification

All personally identifiable information (PII) was removed from the data files by the NSRR team.

## Data overview

### Covariate/Phenotype Datasets (CSV)

Includes demographic information (age, gender, race/ethnicity, athletic status, medications, brain injury severity and cause), and responses to concussion symptom questionnaires (RPQ, PCCS), emotional and cognitive questionnaires (PANAS, CERQ, UPPS-P, BIS/BAS), and sleep quality assessment questionnaires (PSQI, SSS, CSD)

Data for 15 participants are currently available in the dataset.

The harmonized dataset contains the most frequently used demographic variables available in the RESET dataset collected at study visit 1. These variables were curated by the NSRR team to allow ready inter-operability with other NSRR datasets. Key harmonized variables include:
